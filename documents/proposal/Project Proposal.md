**Project Title:** Identifying Cell Types in Lung Tumors: A Machine Learning Approach

**Project Description:**

Cells within a lung tumor can have very different functions. In addition to cancer cells, tumors contain immune and other supporting cells that may influence disease progression. Our project aims to identify these cell types using a public single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) dataset. We will focus on primary lung tumor samples from patients with lung adenocarcinoma, containing expression measurements of 29,634 genes across individual cells. We plan to develop an analysis pipeline covering data preprocessing, feature selection, dimensionality reduction, representation learning, clustering, cell type annotation, and other downstream analyses. We will also compare different representations, including those obtained from PCA and autoencoders, by evaluating how well the resulting clusters align with existing cell-type annotations. This work can help cancer biologists better understand the cellular composition of tumors. It can also support medical researchers studying the role of immune and other surrounding cells in lung cancer. Machine learning is particularly suitable for this problem because scRNA-seq data contain thousands of gene expression features per cell, along with substantial sparsity and noise. Through careful preprocessing and unsupervised learning, we can extract meaningful patterns from high-dimensional gene expression data and identify groups of cells with similar biological characteristics.
